**Memo: Ontario CRA registration, and how Canadian tenant-screening vendors position themselves** (web research, 2026-09-13)

## Q1 — Registering as a consumer reporting agency in Ontario

**Who must register.** *Consumer Reporting Act*, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.33, s. 3: "No person shall conduct or act as a consumer reporting agency or act as a personal information investigator unless registered by the Registrar under this Act." A CRA is "a person who for gain or profit or on a regular co-operative non-profit basis furnishes consumer reports" (s. 1(1)); a consumer report covers "credit information or personal information, or both" used for a s. 8(1)(d) purpose, which expressly includes "(ii) … the entering into or renewal of a tenancy agreement." ([e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c33)). A 2012 Dentons note warns that identity-verification/background-check platforms can cross this line; offences carry corporate fines ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/consumer-law/186882/fine-lines-data-reselling-or-consumer-reporting)). Penalties were raised in Dec 2023: s. 23 now $50,000/1 yr for individuals and directors who "knowingly concur", $250,000 for corporations; new s. 23.1 gives consumers a statutory damages action in Superior Court ([e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c33)).

**Registrar.** Registrar of Consumer Reporting Agencies, Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement (MPBSDP), Consumer Protection Ontario. Complaints go to the Registrar ([ontario.ca/credit-reports](https://www.ontario.ca/page/credit-reports)). Licensing Unit: CPOlicensing@ontario.ca, PO Box 450, Toronto M7A 2J6; 416-326-6203 / 1-800-889-9768 ([forms index](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ministry-and-administrative-authority-application-licence-and-permit-forms); [register page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/search-business-licence-registration-or-appointment)).

**Step-by-step**

1. **Form 06019E** "Application for Registration or Appointment (Business) – New, Reinstatement or Renewal" (shared with Bailiffs/Collection Agencies). Asks business type, every officer/director/shareholder (separate form per corporate shareholder), "Have you ever been convicted of an offence … or are you currently the subject of any charges?", bankruptcy in the past 10 years, other-jurisdiction licences ([form text](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/de42d640-e276-4983-ad13-5f3344bd110a/resource/dacb20ed-0ca5-4de2-86d3-3a8a5ae02e7c/download/txt_06019e.htm); [dataset 007-06019](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/en/dataset/007-06019)). No online portal — email or mail.
2. **Eligibility (Reg. 177 s. 6; info sheet):** 18+, and "at least two years of actual or related experience in all phases of consumer reporting"; "Operate from a permanent place of business in Ontario, which is open to public during normal business hours and is not a residence" ([info sheet](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/d6120ccb-f086-4ed0-b322-a11845ca1c2d/resource/a185d588-fb78-4449-b71e-aa5d905e2231/download/txt_06019e_info.htm); [Reg. 177](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900177)).
3. **Financials:** Reg. 177 s. 7(5) — "A copy of a current financial statement for the applicant that is prepared by an individual licensed in accordance with the Public Accounting Act, 2004 … shall be attached"; first-time applicants provide an opening statement. **No bond:** s. 25(d) of the Act permits bonding regulations, but Reg. 177 prescribes none — financial responsibility is tested under s. 4(1)(a)/(c)(i).
4. **Fee:** Reg. 177 s. 5 — "$290, upon application for registration as a consumer reporting agency or renewal"; $290 per branch; $190 per personal-information investigator. Exempt if licensed as a private investigator under the PSISA 2005 (Reg. 177 s. 2). Payable to Minister of Finance; $35 NSF ([info sheet](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/d6120ccb-f086-4ed0-b322-a11845ca1c2d/resource/a185d588-fb78-4449-b71e-aa5d905e2231/download/txt_06019e_info.htm)).
5. **Clearances:** "A check of the Canadian Police Information Computer is conducted on all applications" (info sheet); the form authorises consultation with regulators, the Registrar of Bankruptcies and credit bureaus. Grounds to refuse (s. 4(1)): financial irresponsibility, or "the past conduct of its officers or directors affords reasonable grounds for belief that its business will not be carried on in accordance with law and with integrity and honesty."
6. **Decision / appeal:** Registrar may refuse, suspend, revoke or refuse renewal (s. 5) only after a notice of proposal with reasons; 15 days to demand a Licence Appeal Tribunal hearing (s. 6). Re-application on new evidence (s. 7). Registration is non-transferable (s. 4(4)).
7. **Term & renewal:** Reg. 177 s. 7(1): "Every registration expires on the date shown on the certificate of registration unless an application for renewal … together with the appropriate fee … is filed with the Registrar prior to the date of expiry"; s. 6(8) deems continuance while a timely renewal is pending. No term is fixed in regulation; the public register shows two-year certificates for recent registrants (Openroom registered 2025-07-09, expiry 2027-07-09) and three-year spans for some others (LCB to 2028-05-27).
8. **Processing time:** not published. Openroom describes the ministry assessing its products "over the last year" before certifying on 2025-07-09 ([Openroom newsletter](https://newsletter.openroom.ca/p/government-certified-now)).
9. **Public register:** yes. Search by business type/licence number at [ontario.ca search page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/search-business-licence-registration-or-appointment) (updated monthly), and the open-data CSV "Select Licence and Registration Data" ([data.ontario.ca](https://data.ontario.ca/en/dataset/select-licence-and-registration-data); [July 2026 CSV](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/5f0c3532-6e42-4ed7-a92c-ecde22bfea06/resource/5a4f44a7-c656-4977-b4d0-91bedaa0ea06/download/mpbsdp-licensed_businesses-en-utf8-2026-08-04_f.csv)). The July 2026 file lists **34 CRAs**, all "Issued" — Equifax, TransUnion, Nova Credit, First Advantage, Sterling, HireRight, KPMG Forensic, and the tenant-screening names below. Enforcement postings go to the Consumer Beware List ([CPO complaint notice](https://files.ontario.ca/cpo_0008-17_cpo_notices_consumer_reporting.pdf)).

|---|---|

**Province-specific?** Yes. There is no federal CRA licence (PIPEDA governs privacy only). Ontario registration is required for anyone furnishing reports "for gain" (s. 3); Saskatchewan licenses under its *Credit Reporting Act* ([FCAA](https://fcaa.gov.sk.ca/regulated-businesses-persons/businesses/credit-reporting-agencies/general-information)); Quebec requires "personal information agents" to register with the CAI ([Gowling](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2024/personal-information-agents)); BC regulates conduct under BPCPA Part 6 but lists no CRA licence ([Consumer Protection BC](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/consumer-help/credit-reporting/)); Alberta's licensing page has no reporting-agency licence ([Alberta](https://www.alberta.ca/consumer-licensing-forms)). LCB itself says "Many provinces do not require LCB to be provincially registered" while it is "registered in Ontario … under 2642985 Ontario Inc." ([LCB](https://landlordcreditbureau.ca/legal-framework-cdn/)).

**Agency and user at once?** Nothing in the Act prohibits it; SingleKey is both a registered CRA and a data furnisher to Equifax ([SingleKey](https://www.singlekey.com/en-ca/tenant-report/singlekey-is-now-a-credit-reporting-agency/)). The constraints are functional: as agency you carry ss. 8–13; as user you carry ss. 10–11.

## Five recommendations for Stayloop

1. **Register in Ontario now.** Stayloop furnishes, for a fee, compiled credit and personal information for tenancy decisions — squarely the s. 1/s. 8(1)(d)(ii) definition. Every direct competitor operating a proprietary report (SingleKey, Certn, Openroom, LCB, RentCheck) is on the register; being the unregistered one is the s. 23/23.1 exposure. Budget: $290, accountant-prepared financial statement, a non-residential Ontario office, CPIC checks on principals, and a documented two-year consumer-reporting experience narrative (hire/contract if needed). Expect ~a year of ministry review (Openroom's experience).

2. **Re-engineer the report to s. 9(3).** The forensics/LTB/court layers generate "unfavourable personal information": each such item must carry a corroboration note or an explicit "not corroborated" flag (s. 9(3)(b)); enforce the 7-year and 12-month writ/action rules in code (CanLII/LTB hits older than 7 years, or actions >12 months old without current status, must be suppressed); never emit protected-ground data (s. 9(3)(l)) — the existing OHRC guardrail should become a hard filter on report output, not just agent turns.

3. **Build the s. 12/13 consumer channel before launch.** A free, electronic "my file" endpoint (2-business-day SLA), listing every accessor (3 yrs) and recipient (1 yr), plus a dispute workflow that records "best endeavours" and re-notifies landlords who received the report in the prior 60 days. The current `/screening/[id]/notice` letter is a good start but is a user-side (s. 10(7)) document, not the agency-side disclosure.

4. **Make landlords compliant users.** Bake s. 10(2)'s pre-request written notice (bold/underlined, ≥10 pt) into the applicant invite, and gate report release on the landlord's tenancy-purpose attestation (s. 11(3)). Ship a s. 10(7) adverse-action template naming Stayloop as the CRA with the 60-day request window.

5. **Keep the Equifax pull and the AI compilation legally separate.** The applicant-authorised Equifax pull is a s. 8(1)(b) "written instructions of the consumer" disclosure; keep that consent versioned and scoped (already done). Store nothing from it that the s. 12 file cannot reproduce, and do not treat the model's narrative as "corroboration" — corroboration must cite a source document or public record. Given Certn's still-open OPC investigation and the OPC's 2016 finding that a landlord "bad tenant list" required CRA licensing ([OPC](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2016/pipeda-2016-002/)), registration plus these controls is also the strongest PIPEDA posture.

Caveats: e-Laws consolidation current to 9 Sep 2026; register CSV is the July 2026 snapshot; FrontLobby's OPC "confirmation" rests on its own release; Rentify's site was unreadable.

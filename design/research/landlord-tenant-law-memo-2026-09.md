# Ontario tenant / landlord rights and platform obligations — research memo (2026-09-13)

Primary-source research by a review agent; every claim carries its URL. Companion to `design/roles-and-agent-verification-2026-09.md`.

**Memo: Ontario tenant/landlord rights and the legal requirements they impose on Stayloop** (web research, 2026-09-13; every claim carries a primary URL where one could be reached; e-Laws pages were read in the browser because they are JS-rendered)

## 1. Tenant

**Rights**
- Equal treatment in occupancy of accommodation without discrimination on 16 grounds (race, ancestry, place of origin, colour, ethnic origin, citizenship, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, age, marital status, family status, disability, receipt of public assistance) — Human Rights Code s.2(1); "no person shall infringe or do, directly or indirectly, anything that infringes" (s.9); constructive discrimination s.11(1) — a neutral "requirement, qualification or factor" that excludes a Code-protected group infringes unless "reasonable and bona fide", and s.11(2) says that cannot be found unless accommodation would cause undue hardship. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19
- No fees: RTA s.134(1)(a) — no landlord shall "collect or require or attempt to collect or require from a tenant, prospective tenant or former tenant… a fee, premium, commission, bonus, penalty, key deposit or other like amount of money whether or not the money is refundable"; s.134(2) extends this to "superintendent, property manager or other person who acts on behalf of a landlord". Application fees are therefore illegal. Fines: s.238 — up to $100,000 (individual) / $500,000 (corporation). https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06r17
- Deposits: s.105(1) "The only security deposit that a landlord may collect is a rent deposit"; s.106(2) capped at the lesser of one rent period / one month; s.106(6) annual interest at the guideline rate. No damage deposit (Ontario guide: "Damage deposits: Prohibited"). https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06r17 · https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-ontarios-standard-lease
- No forced post-dated cheques or automatic debits (s.108). Same URL.
- Standard lease: s.12.1(1) — tenancy agreements of the prescribed class "shall be in the form prescribed"; s.12.1(2) must be signed "on or before the day the tenant is entitled to occupy"; s.12(2) landlord must give a signed copy within 21 days; s.12(4) until then rent obligation is suspended; s.12.1(5)–(7) if no standard form, tenant may demand one, and after 21 days withhold up to one month's rent; s.47.0.1 tenant may terminate a fixed term on 60 days' notice. Same URL.
- Landlord duties owed to tenant: repair and habitability s.20(1); no substantial interference s.22; no harassment s.23. Same URL.
- Right to know what was used against them: Consumer Reporting Act s.10(7) — where "a benefit is denied… because of information received from a consumer reporting agency or a person other than a consumer reporting agency, the user of such information shall deliver to the consumer at the time such action is communicated… notice of the fact" and, on request within 60 days, disclose "the nature and source of the information" (non-agency) or the agency's name and address; the notice must state that right. s.12: right to obtain one's file, score, sources, and recipients from an agency. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c33
- Privacy: PIPEDA gives access and correction rights (Sched. 1 cl. 4.9, 4.9.5) and retention limits (4.5.3 "destroyed, erased, or made anonymous"). https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/page-7.html
- No statutory right to *reasons* for refusal was found (see "could not confirm").

**Responsibilities**: ordinary cleanliness (s.33); repair of "undue damage… caused by the wilful or negligent conduct of the tenant" or guests (s.34); no harassment of landlord (s.36); conduct that "substantially interferes with the reasonable enjoyment" is a termination ground (s.64); knowing material misrepresentation of income is a termination ground only in RGI/social housing (s.60). https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06r17

**What may be asked at application**: O. Reg. 290/98 s.1(1): "credit references and rental history information, or either of them… and… authorization to conduct credit checks"; s.1(3) income information only if the s.1(1) items are also requested; s.2 a guarantee for rent and a s.105/106 rent deposit. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980290 · OHRC: "Regulation 290/98 under the Code permits no other inquiries". https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-human-rights-and-rental-housing
OPC (page updated 2026-06-18): landlords must comply with PIPEDA; SIN — "No… The OPC generally recommends that individuals do not give the SIN to a private-sector organization unless there is a legal requirement"; a credit check needs name, address, date of birth; landlords "should not collect more information than they need"; copying licences/pay stubs typically exceeds need. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/landlords-and-tenants/privacy-in-the-landlord-and-tenant-relationship/ · OPC early-resolved case 2013-01: SIN, licence and banking fields must be marked optional. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/ser/2013/er_001_130425/

## 2. Landlord

- Permitted checks: O. Reg. 290/98 s.1(2) — may consider credit references, rental history and credit checks "alone or in any combination" and "select or refuse… accordingly"; s.1(4) income only "together with all the other information"; s.1(5) income alone only if nothing else was obtained; s.3 RGI housing exempt; s.4 nothing authorizes refusal on a Code ground. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980290 Code s.21(3) shields these practices only "in the manner prescribed". https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19
- OHRC: "It is illegal for housing providers to apply a rent-to-income ratio such as a 30% cut-off rule" (except subsidized housing); "A lack of rental or credit history should not be viewed negatively"; guarantor "only if the landlord has the same requirements for all tenants, not just for people identified by Code grounds, such as recent immigrants or people receiving social assistance"; *Kearney v. Bramalea* — minimum-income criteria failed because landlords "could not show that the use of the criteria was reasonable and bona fide"; *Ahmed* — treating no rental history like bad history is discrimination where linked to a Code ground. https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-human-rights-and-rental-housing · https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/human-rights-housing-overview-landlords-brochure
- Credit-check consent: 290/98 s.1(1) frames it as "authorization"; CRA s.8(1)(d)(ii) lets an agency furnish a report to someone who "intends to use the information in connection with the entering into or renewal of a tenancy agreement"; s.10(2) forbids obtaining a report containing *personal information* without prior written notice; s.11 requires written notice to anyone about whom information is requested; s.10(6) notices in bold/underlined ≥10-pt. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c33
- Standard lease / deposits / copy: as in §1 (RTA ss.12, 12.1, 105–106). Guide: standard lease required for most private residential tenancies signed on/after 2018-04-30; exempt: care homes, mobile home parks, land-lease, most social housing, co-ops; s.15 additional terms allowed, but "Any extra term which attempts to take away a right or responsibility under the act is void". https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-ontarios-standard-lease
- PIPEDA duties: identify purposes, consent, limit collection, safeguard, provide access, use only for stated purposes; do not retain indefinitely; "do not request SIN numbers"; "landlords do not have the right to disclose information such as a poor payment history to an unregulated or ad hoc 'bad tenants list'". https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/landlords-and-tenants/privacy-in-the-landlord-and-tenant-relationship/ · https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/landlords-and-tenants/02_05_d_66_tips/
- Using a third-party service: Sched. 1 cl. 4.1.3 — the organization remains responsible and "shall use contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection while the information is being processed by a third party"; accuracy cl. 4.6.1. https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/page-7.html
- After refusal: CRA s.10(7) notice duty (above) applies to the *user* of the information, i.e. the landlord, whether the source is an agency or "a person other than a consumer reporting agency". No RTA/Code duty to give reasons found; but under Code s.11 the landlord bears the burden of proving criteria "reasonable and bona fide" if challenged. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19

## 3. Consumer Reporting Act — does Stayloop cross the line?

Definitions (s.1(1)): "'consumer report' means a written, oral or other communication by a consumer reporting agency of credit information or personal information, or both, pertaining to a consumer for consideration in connection with a purpose set out in clause 8 (1) (d)"; "'consumer reporting agency' means a person who for gain or profit or on a regular co-operative non-profit basis furnishes consumer reports"; "'credit information' means information about a consumer as to name, age, occupation, place of residence, previous places of residence, marital status, spouse's name and age, number of dependants, particulars of education or professional qualifications, places of employment, previous places of employment, estimated income, paying habits, outstanding debt obligations, cost of living obligations and assets"; "'personal information' means information other than credit information about a consumer's character, reputation, health, physical or personal characteristics or mode of living or about any other matter concerning the consumer". s.3: "No person shall conduct or act as a consumer reporting agency… unless registered". Agency duties: s.9(1) "all procedures reasonable for ensuring accuracy and fairness"; s.9(3) no unfavourable personal information without corroboration efforts, judgments only with creditor name/amount, seven-year limits. Penalties s.23: $50,000/1 yr (individual), $250,000 (corporation). https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c33

Analysis: the definition turns on *furnishing* credit/personal information about a consumer to a third party, for gain, for a s.8(1)(d) purpose (tenancy is one). Stayloop is paid, and delivers to a landlord a compiled report on paying habits, income, employment, residence history and court/LTB records — every element of "credit information" and "personal information" is present. The fact that the inputs are applicant-supplied or public does not appear in the definition. Precedents: the OPC treated a property manager's shared "bad tenant" list as an unlicensed credit-reporting function and found the purpose not appropriate under PIPEDA s.5(3) (PIPEDA 2016-002). https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2016/pipeda-2016-002/ Openroom, which only surfaces public LTB/court orders, states it "operates as a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) license under the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement in Ontario". https://openroom.ca/tenant-screening/ Certn is under joint OPC/BC/AB investigation (announced 2024-06-04) for tenant-screening accuracy and appropriateness of purposes. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2024/an_240604/ Conclusion: on the statutory text, a paid platform furnishing compiled applicant reports to landlords should assume it is a consumer reporting agency and either register or restructure (e.g., landlord as the sole collector with Stayloop as processor under cl. 4.1.3, and no cross-landlord retention or reuse). This is a legal question that needs counsel; the text points toward registration.

## 4. LTB and court records

- LTB final orders are published on the Open Data Catalogue (Jan–Jun 2026 now; 2021+ in phases; monthly), excluding confidentiality orders, "to ensure easy access and support transparency". https://tribunalsontario.ca/2026/07/24/ltb-operational-update-increasing-public-access-to-final-ltb-orders/ · https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/ltb-order-catalogue
- Licence: Open Government Licence – Ontario permits copy/adapt/commercial use with attribution, but "does not authorize" use of Personal Information or contravention of privacy law. https://www.ontario.ca/page/open-government-licence-ontario So searching is lawful; the *use* of the personal information in it is governed by PIPEDA (consent, accuracy, appropriate purpose) and, if furnished for gain, the CRA (s.9(3)(c)–(d) rules on judgments; the catalogue has no outcome field).
- Blacklists: OPC 2016-002 — ad hoc lists are not an appropriate purpose and lack meaningful consent; standard "credit reports or other information as may be deemed necessary" clauses do not give consent. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2016/pipeda-2016-002/ OPC 2025 review of FrontLobby/LCB (secondary source, press release quoting OPC): debts may be reported to a *registered* agency without consent for collection; positive rent reporting needs opt-in. https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/privacy-commissioner-confirms-frontlobby-s-compliance-setting-a-national-standard-for-rent-reporting-842509922.html
- CanLII terms (site blocks automated fetch; quoted via secondary): prohibit "the bulk or systematic downloading of CanLII Works, including by way of programmatic means" and masking the source by "framing, re-use of search processes"; CanLII sued Caseway (BCSC, 2024-11-04) for scraping. https://www.torkin.com/insights/publication/legality-of-data-scraping-using-ai-revisiting-in-canada · https://www.canlii.org/en/info/terms.html

## 5. AI-assisted decisions

- No federal AI statute: AIDA (Bill C-27) died on prorogation 2025-01-06; ISED's AIDA page is archived (modified 2025-12-09); no successor bill as of June 2026. https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/innovation-better-canada/en/artificial-intelligence-and-data-act · https://srinstitute.utoronto.ca/news/whats-next-for-aida
- Ontario: IPC–OHRC "Principles for the Responsible Use of AI" (2026-01-21): valid/reliable, safe, privacy-protective, human-rights-affirming, transparent, accountable; individuals "should be informed whether and when their personal information is being used in… an AI system"; notify people "when any information presented to them has been generated by AI systems"; "at least a right of review for automated decision processes that do not involve high risk, and the choice of opting out of high-risk automated decision processes"; human-in-the-loop. Aimed at institutions, no housing example. https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/principles-responsible-use-artificial-intelligence OHRC/LCO Human Rights AI Impact Assessment tool (2024). https://www3.ohrc.on.ca/en/human-rights-ai-impact-assessment
- Substantively, Code s.11 already governs: any model factor with disparate impact on a Code group must be "reasonable and bona fide". https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19 OPC meaningful-consent guidelines: emphasize what is collected, with whom shared, purposes, risks of harm; yes/no options. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/collecting-personal-information/consent/gl_omc_201805/

## 6. E-signatures and lease drafting

- Electronic Commerce Act, 2000: s.3(1) nothing requires a person to accept electronic form "without the person's consent" (consent may be inferred from conduct, s.3(2)); s.4 electronic documents not invalid "by reason only of being in electronic form"; s.5 writing requirement satisfied if "accessible so as to be usable for subsequent reference"; s.11(1) "a legal requirement that a document be signed is satisfied by an electronic signature", with reliability conditions s.11(3) for prescribed classes; retention s.12(1) satisfied if kept in the same or an accurate format and accessible; s.31 exclusions are wills, trusts by will, powers of attorney, negotiable instruments, documents of title — leases are not excluded. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/00e17 Ontario guide: "The landlord(s) or tenant(s) can sign the lease electronically if they both agree." https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-ontarios-standard-lease
- Retain: the signed agreement in a format that "accurately represents" it, plus proof of the 21-day copy delivery (RTA s.12(2)) and of consent to electronic form.
- Drafting: Law Society Act s.1(5) legal services = "the application of legal principles and legal judgment with regard to the circumstances or objectives of a person"; s.1(6) para 2 includes selecting, drafting, completing or revising "on behalf of a person" a document affecting "interests in or rights to or in real or personal property"; s.26.1(1) non-licensees may not provide legal services; s.26.2 fines $25,000/$50,000; s.1(8) exemptions include a person "acting on his or her own behalf". https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90l08 Filling the mandatory government form with user-entered facts is form completion by the parties themselves; an AI that composes bespoke Section 15 terms tailored to a landlord's objectives is on the s.1(6) side of the line.

## Requirements for the platform

**Tenant account — may / must be told**
- May: upload documents, give/withdraw versioned consent, see and dispute every data element used (CRA s.10(7), PIPEDA 4.9), request the full file and who received it (CRA s.12), and decline SIN with no penalty.
- Must be told, before collection: exactly what is collected, from where (bureau, court portal, LTB catalogue, bank), who sees it, purposes, retention period, that AI is used and that a human decides (IPC–OHRC), how to correct/dispute (privacy contact), and that refusal/termination is not automated.
- Must never be asked to pay any application, screening or key fee (RTA s.134) — a "tenant pays" unlock link is only lawful if entirely voluntary and never a condition of the tenancy; treat as high-risk and gate with explicit non-condition wording.

**Landlord account — may / must be told**
- May request only: credit references, rental history, credit-check authorization, and income *with* those (O. Reg. 290/98 s.1); a guarantor and one month's rent deposit (s.2). UI must not offer other fields (SIN, marital/family status, citizenship, health).
- Must be told/enforced: no rent-to-income cut-offs or "no credit history = fail" (OHRC); same guarantor rule for everyone; on refusal, deliver the s.10(7) notice naming the source and the 60-day request right; give the standard lease within 21 days; no damage deposit, no fees, no post-dated-cheque or auto-debit requirement (RTA ss.105–108, 134); they remain accountable for data given to Stayloop (PIPEDA 4.1.3).

**Platform itself — must / must not**
- Must: decide (with counsel) whether to register as a consumer reporting agency or restructure as landlord's processor; either way meet s.9 accuracy/corroboration standards, mark uncorroborated adverse items, apply seven-year limits and creditor-name rules to judgments; keep an access/correction/dispute channel; retain only for the stated period and destroy (4.5.3); contractually bind sub-processors (bureau, Veriff, Flinks, model vendors); disclose AI use and keep a human decision-maker; run a Code s.11 disparate-impact review of every scoring rule (HRIA tool); attribute LTB data under OGL and never state outcomes the order does not contain.
- Must not: charge or facilitate charging applicants; collect SIN; treat missing history as negative; use rigid ratios; build cross-landlord tenant lists or reuse one landlord's screening for another without fresh consent (OPC 2016-002); bulk-scrape CanLII; auto-generate bespoke lease clauses (Law Society Act s.1(6)) — limit to the prescribed form, fixed government-published wording, and user-authored Section 15 text with a void-term warning; assert "no record" when a source was unavailable.

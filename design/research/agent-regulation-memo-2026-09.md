# Ontario real-estate agent regulation and platform obligations — research memo (2026-09-13)

Primary-source research by a review agent; every claim carries its URL. Companion to `design/roles-and-agent-verification-2026-09.md`.

# Memo: Ontario rules governing real-estate agents and a platform that hosts them (Stayloop)

Sources are primary (e-Laws consolidated text as of 2026-09-09, RECO, TRREB, CREA, OREA, OHRC). One correction to the brief: the **Code of Ethics is now O. Reg. 365/22** (in force 2023-12-01); O. Reg. 580/05 was revoked that day ([e-Laws 580/05](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050580): "This Regulation was revoked on December 1, 2023. (See: O. Reg. 365/22, s. 17)"). The operative rules on advertising, the Information Guide, agreements and remuneration live in **O. Reg. 567/05 (General)**. O. Reg. 579/05 is education/insurance/records only.

## 1. Who may trade in real estate

**Definition of "trade" (TRESA s.1).** "trade" includes a disposition or acquisition of or transaction in real estate by sale, purchase, ... **lease, rental or otherwise** and any offer or attempt to list real estate ... and **any act, advertisement, conduct or negotiation, directly or indirectly, in furtherance of** any disposition, acquisition, transaction, offer or attempt. ([TRESA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02r30)) So arranging rentals and referring a tenant to a landlord for a fee are both "trading" on the plain text.

**Registration categories (s.1, s.4).** Brokerage (entity that "on behalf of others and for compensation or reward ... trades in real estate or holds ... itself out as such"); broker and salesperson (individuals "employed by a brokerage to trade"); "registrant" = any of these. s.4(1): no person shall trade unless registered; s.4(2): an unregistered person shall not "hold himself, herself or itself out as being a brokerage, broker or salesperson" or "perform any of the functions of a brokerage, broker or salesperson". Broker of record duties: O. Reg. 567/05 s.30 (manage brokerage, supervise, deal with non-compliance) ([567/05](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050567)).

**Exemptions (s.5(1)).** Relevant ones: (f) "a full-time salaried employee of a party to a trade if the employee is acting for or on behalf of his or her employer" (s.5(2): an independent contractor is not an employee); (g) lawyers providing legal services; (h) "a person, on the person's own account, in respect of the person's interest in real estate" (a landlord renting their own unit) — unless the trade results from an offer to act for the other party; **(j) "a person who trades in real estate solely for the purpose of arranging leases to which the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 applies."** The word *solely* matters: any act in furtherance of a sale or a non-RTA lease (commercial, most short-term stays) voids it. Property managers are not named; they rely on (f) (if salaried employees of the landlord) or (j) (if rental-only). PRECs are exempt under s.5(3).

**Penalties.** s.40(1)(c): contravening the Act or regulations (other than the Code of Ethics) is an offence; s.40(3): individuals up to **$50,000 and/or two years less a day**, corporations up to **$250,000**; s.41 restitution orders; s.9: no action for remuneration unless registered or exempt when services were rendered. RECO confirms it "investigates complaints made about ... any person who may be unlawfully trading in real estate" ([RECO complaints process](https://www.reco.on.ca/enforcement/about-recos-complaints-process)). Discipline-committee fines for registrants: $50,000 (individual) / $100,000 (brokerage) (s.21).

## 2. Verifying an agent is registered

- **Public register:** [registrantsearch.reco.on.ca](https://registrantsearch.reco.on.ca/). Search modes: Salesperson/broker (first/middle/last name), Brokerage name, Category/city; a checkbox switches to "Unregistered/Formerly Registered (within the past 10 years)" with termination reasons Lapsed / Voluntarily Terminated / Revoked. No search-by-registration-number field is exposed on the form.
- **Fields shown** (RECO consumer page, [Make sure your real estate agent is registered](https://www.reco.on.ca/consumers/make-sure-your-real-estate-agent-is-registered/)): agent or brokerage name, registration number, registration status, expiry date, broker of record and ownership (brokerages), business contact info, plus 5 years of enforcement activity (hearings/discipline, charges/convictions, registrar's proposals, LAT decisions). The statutory minimum is O. Reg. 567/05 s.11 (legal name and registered name; business address/phone/email; registration number and expiry; category; brokerage officers/directors; pending proposals; suspensions; conditions) and s.13(2) (kept public ≥60 months).
- **Terms:** the register's own disclaimer: "This registrant search feature is intended for private, non-commercial use. Any commercial use of the information in whole or in part, directly or indirectly, is specifically forbidden. The information shows charges and disciplinary action in the last 60 months." RECO also disclaims accuracy ("does not represent, warrant, or guarantee" currency/completeness). **No API or bulk download is offered** on either page.
- **Practical identity evidence** a platform can ask for, all of which are verifiable by a human on the register: registered name exactly as it appears (O. Reg. 567/05 s.8: one registered name; s.8(6): may not trade under any other name), registration number, category, employing brokerage's registered name, and the expiry date; and, if you want brokerage confirmation, the broker of record's name (public) — brokers/salespersons may only trade on behalf of their employing brokerage (TRESA s.31(1)).

## 3. Rules for an agent's online presence and for a platform displaying them

**Advertising (O. Reg. 567/05 s.12.1, duplicating former Code s.36; TRESA s.37).**
- s.12.1(1)–(3): registrant must "clearly and prominently disclose the name in which the registrant is registered in all the registrant's advertisements"; a brokerage naming an agent must use the registered name; a broker/salesperson "shall not advertise in any manner unless the advertisement clearly and prominently identifies the brokerage that employs the broker or salesperson, using the name in which the brokerage is registered."
- s.12.1(4): permitted descriptors — brokerage: "brokerage"/"real estate brokerage"; broker of record: "broker of record"; broker: "broker", "real estate broker", "real estate agent"; salesperson: "salesperson", "real estate agent", "real estate salesperson", "sales representative". s.12.1(5): "REALTOR®" (alone or "REALTOR® salesperson/broker") only "if the broker or salesperson ... is a member in good standing of the Canadian Real Estate Association." s.12.1(7): no confusable terms.
- s.12.1(8)–(10): nothing identifying a party, a specific property, or contents of an agreement (incl. price) without written consent — this is the "sold data" rule.
- TRESA s.37: no "false, misleading or deceptive statements in any advertisement"; s.38: registrar can order cessation/retraction and pre-approval.
- RECO Bulletin 5.1 ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/agents-and-brokerages/reco-bulletins/reco-bulletin-5-1-advertising-requirements)): "Advertising" covers "publication on the internet (including websites and social media sites)"; "Short forms and nicknames must not be used"; comparative claims, volume claims, awards and promises must be verifiable with basis stated; on multi-page sites, the descriptor symbol/legend "must appear on every page that refers to agents." Bulletin 5.2 ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/agents-and-brokerages/reco-bulletins/reco-bulletin-5-2-permitted-terms)): teams "trade on behalf of the brokerage"; "Each salesperson, broker, and the brokerage must be properly identified"; PRECs "are prohibited from advertising". Bulletin 5.3 ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/agents-and-brokerages/reco-bulletins/reco-bulletin-5-3-advertising-online)): team/agent sites "are all brokerage websites"; the commonest complaint is missing brokerage name; testimonials/photos of clients need written consent; "Plagiarism and scraping are unethical"; reposting another brokerage's listing requires consent and credit with the originating brokerage's name and contact; and "If a brokerage or agent advertises on a website that is managed or operated by a third party ... the brokerage or agent must ensure that the operator can process change requests promptly" (e.g. brokerage transfers).
- Code of Ethics O. Reg. 365/22 ([link](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/220365)): s.5 accurate, non-misleading representations; s.6 no conduct contravening the Human Rights Code; s.10 no services/opinions/advice to a self-represented party; s.13 written consent for conflicts.

**Disclosures.**
- RECO Information Guide: O. Reg. 567/05 s.13 (registrar publishes it); Bulletin 2.1 ([PDF](https://www.reco.on.ca/getmedia/eb78fed0-df80-4c70-9471-b26f982148ab/RECO-Bulletin-2-1-RECO-Information-Guide.pdf)): "Before an agent provides services to a client or assistance to a self-represented party, the agent must give a copy of the RECO Information Guide" and explain it; acknowledgement not legally required but onus is on the agent. RECO FAQ ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/agents-and-brokerages/tresa-explained/tresa-faqs)): "for both prospective residential and commercial lease clients you are required to provide and explain the RECO Information Guide before providing any services or assistance."
- Representation agreements: 567/05 s.13.3.1 (agreement must include representation), s.13.4 (written agreement must state effective/expiry dates, remuneration method, services, designated representative). RECO, June 2025 ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/news/for-the-record-brokerage-leadership-edition-june2025)): "agents and brokerages have the same obligations under the law in a rental transaction as they would for a purchase or sale"; a representation agreement "must be in place when an agent represents a party in any transaction—including in rental transactions"; services may include "negotiating the terms of the lease, completing the standard Ontario lease agreement, showing the rental property, or screening tenants."
- Self-represented party = "not a client" (567/05 s.2(5)); before assisting one, s.13.1 requires the Information and Disclosure to Self-represented Party form (disclosure of the client relationship, risks, permitted assistance, recommendation of independent advice) and best efforts to get an acknowledgement; Bulletin 2.4 ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/agents-and-brokerages/reco-bulletins/reco-bulletin-2-4-dealing-with-a-self-represented-party)). Multiple representation: 567/05 s.22 (disclosure + written consent).

**Commission / referral fees.**
- TRESA s.30: "No brokerage shall ... (b) employ an unregistered person to perform a function for which registration is required; or (c) pay any remuneration to a person referred to in clause (a) or (b)". s.31(2): a broker/salesperson may accept remuneration for trading only from their employing brokerage. s.36: all remuneration must be an agreed amount and/or percentage of sale or rental price (no net-listing).
- RECO Registrar's Bulletin, Referral fees ([link](https://www.reco.on.ca/registrars-bulletin/referral-fees/)): "Compensation may not be provided to an individual or entity that is not registered ... if the compensation would be considered to be in furtherance of a trade ... (section 30 of the Act) and for the person receiving the compensation (section 4(2)(b) of the Act). The form of compensation does not matter." Between registrants, referral fees are permitted but "must be received by the registrant's brokerage, then disbursed to the registrant," and disclosed. Registrants may *receive* fees for referring clients to non-registrants (lawyers, mortgage brokers) with written disclosure. OREA's bird-dog bulletin says the same ([link](https://www.orea.com/resources/media-room/special-bulletin/2007/February/BirddogfeesviolateREBBA)).
- Financial-benefit disclosure: 567/05 s.23.1 — any direct or indirect benefit a registrant may receive "from another person in connection with services provided ... to a client" must be disclosed to the client with value estimate and conditions; s.23(1): no remuneration unless under signed written agreement (or conveyed accepted offer / showing / introduction).
- **Implication:** a platform that is *not registered and not exempt* cannot lawfully be paid a per-deal referral fee by a brokerage. The one opening is s.5(1)(j): a platform whose trading is *solely* arranging RTA leases is exempt, so paying it is not paying "a person ... to perform a function for which registration is required." That reading is not confirmed by RECO guidance (see gaps).

**Deposits / trust money.** TRESA s.27: every brokerage keeps a designated Ontario trust account, deposits all trust money, keeps it separate and disburses "only in accordance with the terms of the trust"; O. Reg. 579/05 ss.12–14 records, monthly reconciliation, shortfall reporting. A platform holding a tenant's deposit is not a brokerage trust arrangement; under the RTA the only lawful deposit is a rent deposit (last month's rent) (s.105–106) and s.134 bars key money and other charges.

## 4. TRREB / MLS / CREA constraints

- **VOW (TRREB Competition Tribunal FAQ, [PDF](https://trreb.ca/hlfiles/pdf/TREB-FAQs.pdf))**: consumers access data "only ... through a password-protected virtual office website (VOW) operated by a TREB Member"; members and service providers sign the VOW Agreement and Authorized User Agreement; data "cannot be scraped, mined, sold, resold, licensed, reorganized or monetized in any way, including through the sale of derivative products or marketing reports"; "Can non-Members post sold information? ... cannot be used or posted by non-Members without specific authorization from TREB." A seller's "Distribute to Internet Portals = No" removes the listing from IDX, DDF and REALTOR.ca.
- **onlistings.trreb.ca Terms of Use** ([link](https://onlistings.trreb.ca/)): all Listing Content "strictly for the private, non-commercial use by the User"; "Any commercial use ... is forbidden, subject to prior written consent"; no derivative works, no "screen scraping", no framing, no "robot, bot, spider", and no retaining a listing after removal.
- **CREA DDF® Policy & Rules (Jan 2024, [PDF](https://www.crea.ca/files/technology/english/DDFR-Policy-and-Rules-February-2024-ENG.pdf))**: feeds go to Participants (member brokerages/salespersons), Franchisors, "Real Estate Advertising Websites" and "Partner – a non-member company that has entered into an agreement with CREA to display DDF® Listing Content"; Technology Providers operate sites on a member's behalf (onboarding fee $1,500, application at [realtor.ca/ddf/technology-providers](https://www.realtor.ca/ddf/technology-providers)). Display rules (s.6): "Powered by REALTOR.ca" logo linking to the listing (≥90 px), "the listing brokerage name ... prominently displayed ... appear with the Listing Content", CREA watermarks, MLS®/REALTOR® trademark statement on every page, refresh every 24 h and remove stale listings; filters limited to objective criteria incl. "Rentals" (s.7); anti-scraping obligations (s.5(k)).
- **REALTOR.ca Terms of Use** ([link](https://www.realtor.ca/terms-of-use)): content "intended for the private, non-commercial use by individuals"; prohibited: "web scraping", "data scraping", "development, training, fine tuning, or grounding of any software program, model, algorithm, artificial intelligence system"; no framing. CREA sends cease-and-desist letters to scrapers ([CREA Café](https://www.crea.ca/cafe/copyright-scraping-and-you-oh-my/)).
- **Trademarks:** REALTOR® identifies CREA members only, capitals + ®, not a job title, with trademark statement where possible ([CREA](https://www.crea.ca/cafe/using-the-realtor-trademark-in-advertisements/)); firm/team names need CREA licence ([CREA](https://www.crea.ca/cafe/can-i-use-the-realtor-trademark-in-firm-or-team-names/)); MLS® is an adjective ("MLS® System", "MLS® listings"), never "search the MLS" ([CREA](https://www.crea.ca/cafe/how-to-properly-use-the-mls-trademark/)).
- **Market statistics:** every Market Watch / Rental Market Report page carries "Copyright 20xx Toronto Regional Real Estate Board" and the site says "All rights reserved" ([Market Watch](https://trreb.ca/market-data/market-watch/), [Aug-2026 PDF](https://trreb.ca/wp-content/files/market-stats/market-watch/mw2608.pdf), [Q3-2025 rental PDF](https://trreb.ca/wp-content/files/market-stats/rental-reports/rental_report_Q3-2025.pdf)). No published licence permitting third-party republication with attribution was found; the VOW FAQ's ban on "marketing reports" applies to feed data, not to TRREB's own published reports, so quoting headline figures with attribution is a copyright/fair-dealing question, not a TRREB-rules question.

## 5. Rentals specifically

- A landlord may list and screen for their own unit without a registrant (TRESA s.5(1)(h)); a salaried employee may act for the landlord (s.5(1)(f)); a rental-only intermediary is exempt (s.5(1)(j)). Screening tools are governed by O. Reg. 290/98 and OHRC policy: income information may be requested only alongside rental history, credit references and credit checks; "It is illegal for housing providers to apply a rent-to-income ratio such as a 30% cut-off rule" ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-human-rights-and-rental-housing)).
- **RTA s.134(1)** ([RTA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06r17)): no landlord shall "directly or indirectly ... collect or require or attempt to collect or require from a tenant, prospective tenant or former tenant of the rental unit a fee, premium, commission, bonus, penalty, key deposit or other like amount of money whether or not the money is refundable", nor require payment for goods/services as a condition of tenancy; **s.134(2)** extends this to "superintendent, property manager or other person who acts on behalf of a landlord"; s.134(3) bars tenants/sub-lessors from charging for subletting or surrendering. Exemptions (O. Reg. 516/06 s.17, [link](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/060516)): extra/replacement keys at cost, refundable key deposit at replacement cost, NSF bank charges + $20 admin, settlement payments, etc. s.135: tenant may apply to the LTB to recover money collected illegally. Offences: fines up to $100,000 (individual) / $500,000 (corporation) ([Rental housing offences](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rental-housing-offences)).
- Consequence for a platform: a fee charged to a tenant *by or on behalf of the landlord* as a condition of getting the unit (e.g. the landlord passing on an application/verification/referral fee) is caught. A tenant paying for their *own* optional service (their own screening report to reuse, an agent they hire) is not a landlord charge — but the landlord must not require it.

## 6. Consumer-protection posture

- RECO complaints: online form at [complaints.reco.on.ca](https://complaints.reco.on.ca/); RECO cannot award compensation or cancel contracts ([RECO](https://www.reco.on.ca/enforcement/about-recos-complaints-process)). O. Reg. 567/05 s.27: registrar publishes summaries of resolved complaints. The Information Guide is the mandated consumer document ("must be given to a buyer or seller before services or assistance are provided" — [RECO](https://www.reco.on.ca/about/plans-and-publications/reco-information-guide)); OREA: "The information guide prepared by RECO is mandated and is the only acceptable version" ([OREA](https://www.orea.com/advocacy/TRESA-Phase-2-Updates)).
- Consumer Protection Act, 2002 s.2(2)(f)–(g): the CPA "does not apply in respect of ... consumer transactions for the purchase, sale or lease of real property" or "consumer transactions regulated under the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006" ([CPA 2002](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30)). It still applies to a platform's own paid services to consumers (subscriptions, reports). CPA 2023 "IS NOT YET IN FORCE" ([e-Laws 23c23](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23c23)); the ministry's business guide confirms CPA 2002 remains in effect ([link](https://www.ontario.ca/document/business-guide-consumer-protection)).
- Ministry "Consumer Beware List": businesses charged/convicted under ministry-administered consumer laws or unresponsive to two complaint notices ([link](https://www.ontario.ca/page/search-consumer-beware-list)); it is not RECO's register. Ministry sharing-economy guidance: platforms should "disclose what protections and guarantees they offer" and help resolve disputes ([link](https://www.ontario.ca/page/supporting-sharing-economy)).
- No RECO guidance specific to "online platforms hosting agents" exists beyond Bulletin 5.3's third-party-website rule quoted in §3.

## What this means for a platform like Stayloop

**Must**
1. Verify each agent against the RECO register manually (name, registration number, category, brokerage, expiry, status "registered") and re-check at expiry; store the checked-on date. Do not scrape or bulk-query the register (its terms forbid commercial use).
2. Render agent profiles and listings exactly as TRESA advertising rules require: registered name (no nicknames), one permitted descriptor, and the employing brokerage's registered name "clearly and prominently" on every page/card that shows the agent (567/05 s.12.1; Bulletins 5.1/5.3). Show "brokerage" after the brokerage name.
3. Show "REALTOR®" only where the agent attests CREA membership; otherwise use "real estate agent"/"salesperson"/"broker". Never label Stayloop's own listing feed "MLS".
4. Provide a fast path for agents to update/remove profiles on brokerage change (Bulletin 5.3 puts the onus on the agent to ensure the operator "can process change requests promptly").
5. Keep listing data to landlord-supplied content, DDF/Partner-licensed content, or explicitly licensed feeds; no scraping of realtor.ca or TRREB properties; if DDF content is ever shown, meet §6 display rules (Powered-by logo, brokerage name, watermarks, 24 h refresh).
6. Any money that Stayloop collects from a tenant must be for a service the tenant chooses; never let a landlord or agent make a Stayloop fee a condition of tenancy (RTA s.134(1)–(2)).
7. Keep screening logic O. Reg. 290/98-compliant (no stand-alone income ratio; income only together with rental history/credit).

**Should**
8. Badge = "RECO registration verified on <date> (#number, brokerage)" with a link to the public register; treat the badge as a re-verification, not an endorsement.
9. Make agents attest that clients receive the RECO Information Guide and a written representation agreement before showings/applications (RECO says these apply to rentals), and put the reminder in the showing/application flow. Stayloop cannot deliver the Guide on the agent's behalf as a compliance substitute — the agent must "provide and explain" it.
10. Surface the RECO complaint link on every agent profile; publish what protections Stayloop itself offers (ministry sharing-economy guidance).
11. Before charging or receiving referral fees, get a written opinion on whether Stayloop's rental-only activity fits s.5(1)(j) and keep every Stayloop activity *solely* RTA-lease-related (no buyer/seller referrals, no commercial leases) if relying on it.

**Must-not**
12. Do not accept or split brokerage referral/lead fees while unregistered and outside s.5(1)(j) (TRESA s.30, s.4(2)(b); RECO referral bulletin). The form of compensation is irrelevant.
13. Do not let Stayloop staff show units, negotiate lease terms, or present offers for landlords who are not Stayloop's employer (s.4; OREA unregistered-assistant bulletin) unless the activity is squarely rental-only under s.5(1)(j).
14. Do not display sold/leased prices, client names, testimonials or photos of parties without written consents (567/05 s.12.1(8)–(10)).
15. Do not present agents as "verified" beyond what the register shows (RECO disclaims completeness), and do not imply agents are Stayloop's employees or that Stayloop is a brokerage (s.4(2)(a)).
